## Plain-language summary

This article argues that academic knowledge claims should be evaluated primarily by publicly inspectable reasons and evidence, rather than by the private process through which the prose was produced.

## Why it matters

Large language models are increasingly used to draft, translate, edit, and revise academic prose. Many debates about AI-assisted writing focus on how a text was produced. This article argues that, for the epistemic evaluation of academic knowledge claims, what matters most is the public quality of the scholarly record: the argument, evidence, engagement with relevant work, and, where applicable, data, code, methods, and provenance claims.

This does not mean that authorship, credit, accountability, or disclosure are unimportant. Rather, it means that those ethical and institutional questions should not be confused with the justificatory status of the knowledge claim itself.
