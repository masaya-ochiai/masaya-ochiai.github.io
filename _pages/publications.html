Published

Process Essentialism and the Epistemology of Academic Knowledge: Why How We Write Should Not Determine What We Know

Masaya Ochiai
Synthese 207, Article 200 (2026).
DOI: 10.1007/s11229-026-05580-3

Plain-language note: This article argues that academic knowledge claims should be evaluated primarily by publicly inspectable reasons and evidence, rather than by the private process through which the prose was produced.

Accepted for publication

In addition to the Synthese article listed above, the following three separate sole-authored peer-reviewed articles have been accepted for publication:

  • Masaya Ochiai, “Simultaneous Submission Bans as Peer-Review Congestion Control: Why ‘Ethics’ Language Governs a Capacity-Constrained Review System,” Journal of Scholarly Publishing. Accepted for publication.

    Plain-language note: This article examines simultaneous submission bans not merely as an ethical rule, but as a mechanism for managing congestion in a capacity-constrained peer-review system.

  • Masaya Ochiai, “The Third Option: integrating AI training-data licensing into scholarly publishing workflows,” Insights: the UKSG journal. Accepted for publication.

    Plain-language note: This article proposes a way to integrate AI training-data licensing into scholarly publishing workflows.

  • Masaya Ochiai, “Arbitraging the Absolute: The Infinite Cube and the Pricing of Infinity: A Financial Response to W. J. T. Mitchell,” Artology: Studies in the Arts. Accepted for publication.

    Plain-language note: This article connects finance, infinity, and art theory through a response to W. J. T. Mitchell’s Infinite Cube.
